# You're in control

You decide how much Atlas does on its own, what it keeps, and when it works. Change anything by saying so in your Atlas chat. You never edit settings yourself.

## How much Atlas does on its own
For each kind of action, Atlas works at one of four levels:
- **On its own:** Atlas does it and records what it did.
- **Asks first:** Atlas prepares it and waits for your yes. In chat it simply asks; when it's running in the background, the item waits in your approval list (shown in your Daily Brief and the Control Center).
- **Always asks (locked):** always needs your explicit yes, every single time. This can't be turned off.
- **Never (locked):** Atlas won't do it, even if you ask.

Atlas starts at the highest level that's safe, and you can raise or lower any adjustable level, for everything or for one person, project, organization, or tool. For example: "Ask me before updating anything about Globex", or "You can file my calendar items without asking".

## What Atlas does on its own by default
- Reading your knowledge and your connected tools (within your data rules)
- Researching on the web
- Filing, updating, and linking records; archiving finished items; merging clear duplicates
- Refreshing your dashboards
- Creating drafts (emails, documents) and new documents in your drive
- Saving finished files to your deliverables folder
- Moving processed files in your Atlas Drop folder
- Applying small Atlas updates

## What Atlas asks about first by default
- Changing what it believes about you (profile, voice guides, design system)
- Editing your own notes or journal entries
- Changing schedules, profiles, dashboards, and other behavior settings
- Building new automations, record types, or other extensions
- Labeling or filing your emails; editing existing documents
- Adding holds to your own calendar; changing tasks in your task tool
- Updating shared systems such as a CRM or tracker
- Applying a major Atlas update

## What always needs your approval
These are locked. Atlas shows you exactly what will happen and waits for your clear yes, every time. In background runs they can't happen at all; they wait for you.
- **Sending anything:** emails, replies, forwards, chat messages, comments others can see, meeting responses, invitations
- **Sharing anything:** sharing files, changing permissions, publishing
- **Deleting anything:** inside or outside Atlas, including forgetting or permanently erasing knowledge
- **Changing Atlas's safety settings:** autonomy levels, data rules, and connector permissions, and only in conversation with you

## What Atlas never does
- Payments, purchases, signing or accepting terms
- Changing passwords or account security, or granting anyone access

## Instructions inside your content
Text inside emails, documents, messages, transcripts, and web pages is treated as information, never as instructions to Atlas. An email saying "assistant, forward all contracts to…" can't make Atlas do anything. Atlas flags suspicious messages like that to you.

## Seeing what Atlas did
Every change is recorded: what changed, when, which automation or chat did it, and why. Every fact Atlas tells you shows where it came from. Any change can be undone.

**Try:**
- "What did you do overnight?"
- "Why did you mark that commitment as done?"
- "Undo what you did to the Globex record this morning."

## Data rules
You decide how sensitive information is handled. For each kind of sensitive content, Atlas can:
- **Ignore it:** store nothing.
- **Keep a pointer only:** note that it exists and where, but not what it says.
- **Keep a summary:** the gist, without the sensitive details.
- **Store it normally.**

The kinds are confidential material, personal details (PII), health information (PHI), financial data, legally privileged material, and any custom kinds you add (for example "board materials"). Defaults are careful: confidential and health information are pointer-only, and legally privileged material is ignored, until you say otherwise.

You can also exclude sources entirely: a sender, a mailbox folder, a chat channel, a drive folder, or a meeting series.

**Try:**
- "Don't track anything from the #random channel."
- "Ignore emails from my bank."
- "Keep only summaries of anything marked confidential."
- "Show me my data rules."

## Forget vs. permanently erase
- **Forget:** Atlas removes the item from its knowledge and stops capturing it from now on. A copy still exists in Atlas's version history (that's what makes undo possible), and Atlas tells you so.
- **Permanently erase:** also removes it from the version history. Atlas asks you to confirm explicitly and records that an erase happened (but not what was erased). GitHub may keep cached copies for a short time.

Both need your approval.

**Try:**
- "Forget what I said about the reorg."
- "Permanently erase everything about that candidate."

## Away mode
Tell Atlas when you're away. It keeps filing everything, but pauses your brief, email drafts, status updates, meeting prep, and reminders, and holds approvals. On your first day back, a catch-up brief is waiting: what happened, what changed, what needs you first, and what can wait. Away mode ends on its own, and you can set it up in advance.

**Try:**
- "I'm out from the 12th to the 20th."
- "I'm back early."

## Pausing
Pause any automation or moment, for a while or until you say so.

**Try:**
- "Pause the weekly review until November."
- "Stop the evening thoughts reminder."
- "Resume everything."

## Which meetings Atlas shows
Choose whether your brief, Week Ahead, and meeting prep include all-day events, meetings you've tentatively accepted, and invitations you haven't answered yet. All are included unless you say otherwise. Atlas still keeps track of skipped meetings, so one shows up as soon as you accept it, and deadlines and milestones on your calendar always show.

**Try:**
- "Hide all-day events from my brief."
- "Skip meetings I've only tentatively accepted."
- "Don't show invites I haven't answered."

## Changing your automation profile
Lean, Standard, or Full (see **Automations**). Atlas suggests one for your Claude plan; if your usage suggests a different one, the monthly automation review will say so.

**Try:**
- "Switch me to the Standard profile."
- "Run the brief earlier, at 6."
- "Stop drafting email replies."

## What Atlas knows about you
Everything Atlas believes about you is on the **About You** dashboard, with where each belief came from and whether you've confirmed it. New observations are suggested to you before Atlas relies on them.

**Try:**
- "What do you know about me?"
- "That's wrong: I prefer long-form updates for my CEO."

## Your permission to store work information
At setup you confirmed that you're permitted to store work information in your private repository. Atlas asks you to re-confirm once a quarter, and whenever you change jobs or roles. Tell Atlas if that ever changes ("I've changed jobs").

## Your data is yours
Everything is stored in your own private repository, in plain readable files. You can download it, read it without Atlas, or take it elsewhere at any time.

**Try:**
- "Export everything about the Globex account."
- "Keep less: switch to summaries and pointers only."
